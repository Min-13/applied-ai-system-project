General Pet Care Guide

Feeding
Dogs should be fed twice daily, once in the morning and once in the evening. Fresh water should always be available. Avoid overfeeding — obesity is a leading cause of joint and heart problems in pets.
Cats are natural grazers but do better on scheduled meals to prevent overeating. Wet food helps with hydration.
Never feed pets chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic, macadamia nuts, or anything containing xylitol. These are toxic and can be fatal.

Exercise
Dogs need at least 30 minutes of exercise per day. High-energy breeds like Labradors, Border Collies, and Huskies need 60–90 minutes of vigorous activity.
Cats benefit from two 10-minute interactive play sessions daily using toys that simulate hunting. Laser pointers and feather wands are effective.
Senior pets need gentler, shorter exercise sessions. Swimming is low-impact and good for dogs with joint issues.

Grooming
Brush dogs with medium to long coats at least 3 times per week to prevent matting and reduce shedding.
Trim nails every 4–6 weeks. Overgrown nails can cause joint pain and change posture.
Clean ears monthly using a veterinarian-approved cleaner. Signs of infection include odor, redness, discharge, or frequent scratching.
Brush teeth 2–3 times per week using pet-safe toothpaste to prevent dental disease.

Health Monitoring
Weigh your pet monthly. Sudden weight loss or gain can indicate diabetes, thyroid issues, or cancer.
Watch for signs of illness: lethargy, loss of appetite, increased thirst, unusual discharge, limping, or behavioral changes.
Annual wellness exams are recommended for adult pets. Senior pets (7+ years) should be seen every 6 months.

Vaccinations and Preventatives
Keep vaccinations current. Core vaccines for dogs include rabies, distemper, and parvovirus. Core vaccines for cats include rabies and FVRCP.
Use monthly flea, tick, and heartworm preventatives year-round, especially in warm climates.

Emergency Signs
Seek immediate veterinary care if your pet shows: difficulty breathing, seizures, collapse, pale or blue gums, suspected poisoning, severe vomiting or diarrhea lasting more than 24 hours, inability to urinate, eye injuries, or trauma from an accident.
When in doubt, call your vet or an emergency animal hospital immediately. Do not wait.
